# Lighting and Texture / 光影与质感参考

Choose light, color and depth for the visual medium, story information and source style. None of the following is mandatory on every shot.

## 1. Describe the light that matters

Consider source and direction, hard/soft quality, color relationship, shadow shape and material response.
A portrait may benefit from sculpted side light; an environment reveal may need broad illumination; graphic animation may use flat, designed shadow shapes.
Contact shadows, reflections and highlights matter when they make weight, contact or material readable.

Do not require half-face lighting, four highlight locations, catchlights or shallow depth on every character frame.
No single missing lighting term proves the output will be flat.

## 2. Select by medium

| Medium | Relevant controls |
|---|---|
| 2D comic/animation | Shadow grouping, edge/line behavior, graphic light shapes, palette hierarchy |
| 3D animation | Designed shading, material response, readable volumes and contact |
| Live action | Motivated sources, tonal separation, skin/material response and plausible exposure |
| Ink/painterly/stop motion | Marks, surface, pigment/material and style-specific depth |

A generic “no anime/cartoon/CGI” library can contradict the target medium. Filter exclusions before using them.

## 3. Optional photographic recipes

| Recipe | Visible property | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Side / Rembrandt-like | One side carries more shape and shadow | Direction benefits from sculpted volume |
| High frontal / butterfly-like | Symmetric facial emphasis | The chosen source and pose call for it |
| Broad diffuse | Soft transitions | Calm environment or inspectable asset |
| Rim/back light | Edge separation | Silhouette or material needs separation |
| Hard directional | Distinct shadow geometry | Graphic staging or tension needs it |

Do not choose the setup by gender. Do not stack recipes without deciding which source supplies what.

## 4. Color and source identity

Define major color relationships and which objects carry them. HEX values can communicate intent, but output compliance must be observed.
For black and white, specify tonal separation; color words can be translated into light/dark relationships.
Film stock, cinematographer credits, halation, grain and source defects are optional references. Describe their observable purpose and test whether they help.

## 5. Depth and material

Choose focus depth according to readable story layers. Background blur can isolate a face; deep focus can reveal a watcher behind it.
Material details belong where the camera can show them. Pores do not belong in every animation style, and every prop does not need a commercial product-lighting setup.
Reflections/contact should follow the selected stylization and physical logic.

## 6. Continuity

Record light sources in world space; camera-relative left/right can change with a reverse angle.
Copying the same sentence does not prove light consistency. Inspect actual shadow direction, exposure and color across the cut.
When a light changes intentionally, record its cause and the desired timing.

## 7. Iteration

If actual output is too flat, identify missing separation, value grouping or material response before adding terms.
If output is cluttered, reduce competing accents and visual emphasis.
Compare changes on the selected model/task and retain the result as evidence.
